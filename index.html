Our Mission

MCA exists to develop people while strengthening the communities and wild places around us. Through meaningful challenge, service, stewardship, and community, we create opportunities for people to learn, lead, and act.

Mountaineering is not the end goal; it is one of the environments where this work comes to life—where people rely on one another, discover what they are capable of, and take responsibility for something beyond themselves.

We believe personal growth and community impact go hand in hand: people become better by helping make the world around them better.

Our Story

I wanted to get into mountaineering, but I didn’t see any clear pathways. It was hard to get into safely and affordably, and then it was hard to find other people willing to go with me. That was the original problem that led me to believe a club at ASU could help myself and others.

But there are also a lot of other changes I want to see in the world. The way I think is: well, let’s combine them (under the club). I work a lot with kids with disabilities, and I strongly believe they deserve access to nature—and to the challenge, hardship, and growth that can be found there—as much as anyone else. We have the ability to help create that access. I began to see the intersection: our mountaineering club could lend its manpower and energy to this initiative and help make those experiences possible.

Then, as “I” became “we,” a lot of new passions got thrown in—and that became our common ground. That passion is what continues to drive us forward.

So, we are becoming a network of driven people trying to make a difference, while helping one another make the differences each of us wants to see.

Our Philosophy

We believe that wild places possess inherent value and that every living being possesses inherent worth. Because something possesses inherent value, it deserves our care, respect, and stewardship.

We believe that people flourish through purpose, meaningful challenge, strong community, and time spent in nature. These experiences cultivate resilience, deepen relationships, strengthen character, and foster a lasting sense of responsibility.

We also believe that caring for others and caring for ourselves are not opposing goals—they reinforce one another. Likewise, the well-being of people and the well-being of the places they inhabit are deeply connected. When we improve the world around us, we also shape the kind of people we become.

Therefore, we seek experiences that simultaneously strengthen people and strengthen their relationship with the natural world.

We believe mountaineering provides one of the richest environments in which to cultivate these values. Time spent in the backcountry asks us to rely on one another, embrace meaningful challenge, act with responsibility, and experience the beauty and fragility of wild places firsthand. Through shared challenge, shared responsibility, and shared accomplishment, we build stronger communities while developing a deeper appreciation for the landscapes that sustain us.

If contributing to something larger than ourselves gives life meaning, then when we have the ability to improve something, we should. Every expedition is an opportunity to leave a trail cleaner, teach a new skill, support a teammate, protect a wild place, or strengthen the community around us. These actions are not separate from mountaineering—they are part of it.

The Mountaineering Club exists to turn these beliefs into action. We climb not simply to reach summits, but to become healthier, more capable, and more connected people. Through adventure, stewardship, and service, we strive to leave every person, every place, and every community better than we found it.

Leadership Opportunities

Explore current leadership positions, responsibilities, expectations, and ways to express interest.

Team Lead

Team Leads make the club feel smaller, more personal, and more dependable. They help members move from simply attending events to preparing for and completing a meaningful shared objective.

Responsibilities

  • Lead a small team of members throughout the semester.
  • Plan and organize one major trip or expedition.
  • Prepare members through training, communication, and trip planning.
  • Coordinate transportation, gear, route information, and emergency planning.
  • Attend regular leadership meetings.
  • Document plans, contacts, and lessons for future leaders.

Good fit for someone who…

  • Enjoys helping people grow.
  • Can communicate consistently.
  • Is organized and dependable.
  • Takes safety seriously.
  • Is willing to ask for help when needed.

Time commitment

Roughly 2–4 hours during a normal week. More time around training days and the team’s major trip.

Experience needed

Some outdoor experience is helpful. You do not need to be the strongest climber or most experienced mountaineer. You do need to be honest about your limits and willing to keep learning.

President

The President keeps the different parts of MCA moving in the same direction. The role is less about personally doing everything and more about making sure leaders have what they need to succeed.

Responsibilities

  • Provide overall direction for the club.
  • Coordinate officers and Team Leads.
  • Run leadership meetings and oversee major club decisions.
  • Maintain communication with ASU, advisors, and outside organizations.
  • Make sure responsibilities are assigned and followed through.
  • Protect the club’s continuity, records, and long-term direction.

Good fit for someone who…

  • Communicates clearly.
  • Can make decisions without shutting others out.
  • Remains calm when plans change.
  • Is comfortable holding people accountable.
  • Cares about the whole organization rather than one specific program.

Time commitment

Around 4–7 hours most weeks. More during major events, recruitment, elections, and administrative deadlines.

Experience needed

Previous leadership experience is strongly helpful. Familiarity with club operations and MCA’s programs is important.

Vice President

The Vice President helps prevent good ideas from getting lost between planning and execution. It is a flexible role that responds to whatever the organization most needs.

Responsibilities

  • Support the President and other officers.
  • Help coordinate meetings, projects, and internal communication.
  • Track unfinished work and help leaders follow through.
  • Step in when the President is unavailable.
  • Help solve operational problems across the club.
  • Support major events and semester planning.

Good fit for someone who…

  • Notices what needs to be done.
  • Enjoys solving practical problems.
  • Can work across several different projects.
  • Communicates well with different personalities.
  • Is dependable without needing constant direction.

Time commitment

Around 3–5 hours most weeks. May increase around major events.

Experience needed

Prior leadership or project-management experience is helpful. Strong organization and communication matter more than technical outdoor skill.

Treasurer

The Treasurer helps MCA make ambitious plans without becoming financially irresponsible. This role protects both the club and its members by keeping money organized and transparent.

Responsibilities

  • Maintain financial records and budgets.
  • Manage reimbursements and purchase documentation.
  • Help leaders estimate costs for trips and events.
  • Track funding, dues, grants, donations, and fundraising income.
  • Help develop financial-assistance and gear-access programs.
  • Provide clear financial reports to club leadership.

Good fit for someone who…

  • Is careful and detail-oriented.
  • Is comfortable working with numbers.
  • Keeps good records.
  • Communicates honestly about financial limits.
  • Wants to improve access and affordability.

Time commitment

Around 2–4 hours most weeks. More during budgeting periods, reimbursements, and fundraising.

Experience needed

No accounting background is required. Spreadsheet skills and comfort learning ASU financial procedures are important.

Trips Director

The Trips Director helps transform exciting ideas into realistic plans. The role does not replace individual trip leaders; it gives them structure, support, and another set of eyes.

Responsibilities

  • Coordinate the semester trip calendar.
  • Support Team Leads and trip organizers.
  • Review route plans, logistics, permits, equipment, transportation, and emergency information.
  • Help determine whether proposed trips match member readiness.
  • Reduce scheduling conflicts between major trips.
  • Maintain trip-planning templates and records.

Good fit for someone who…

  • Loves route research and planning.
  • Thinks carefully about logistics.
  • Can identify missing information.
  • Balances ambition with realism.
  • Enjoys helping other people lead.

Time commitment

Around 2–4 hours most weeks. More before major expeditions and when building the semester calendar.

Experience needed

Meaningful hiking, backpacking, climbing, or trip-planning experience is helpful. Must be willing to research unfamiliar objectives rather than rely only on personal experience.

Training Director

The Training Director helps members prepare before they enter more serious environments. The goal is not to pretend every workshop creates an expert, but to give members practical foundations and clear next steps.

Responsibilities

  • Plan clinics, workshops, and recurring training events.
  • Connect training to upcoming club trips.
  • Organize conditioning events such as StairMaster sessions.
  • Find qualified instructors and trusted educational resources.
  • Help create beginner-friendly pathways into outdoor skills.
  • Maintain training materials for future leaders.

Good fit for someone who…

  • Enjoys teaching.
  • Can explain skills clearly.
  • Is patient with beginners.
  • Cares about preparation and safety.
  • Knows when an outside instructor is needed.

Time commitment

Around 2–4 hours most weeks. More during workshops or before major trips.

Experience needed

Experience with some outdoor skills is important. Certifications are valuable but not necessarily required for every training topic. The leader must stay honest about the limits of their expertise.

Service Coordinator

The Service Coordinator turns the club’s desire to help into organized, reliable action. This role makes sure partners can trust MCA to show up prepared and follow through.

Responsibilities

  • Plan and schedule service projects.
  • Coordinate projects involving kids with disabilities, shelter-dog walks, Papago cleanup, and restoration work.
  • Build relationships with nonprofits, parks, shelters, and community organizations.
  • Prepare volunteers with clear expectations, supplies, and safety information.
  • Track volunteer hours and project impact.
  • Help service remain a consistent part of MCA rather than an occasional addition.

Good fit for someone who…

  • Cares deeply about people, animals, and outdoor places.
  • Communicates professionally.
  • Is comfortable coordinating volunteers.
  • Follows through on commitments.
  • Enjoys building community partnerships.

Time commitment

Around 2–4 hours during planning weeks. Additional time during the service projects themselves.

Experience needed

No specialized outdoor experience is required. Volunteer, disability-support, nonprofit, or event-planning experience is helpful.

Partnerships Director

The Partnerships Director helps MCA gain access to resources and opportunities it could not create alone. The emphasis should be on real relationships, not simply asking companies for free gear.

Responsibilities

  • Build relationships with sponsors, nonprofits, outdoor organizations, instructors, and local businesses.
  • Develop partnership proposals and outreach messages.
  • Seek gear support, discounts, funding, training, and service opportunities.
  • Maintain ongoing communication with existing partners.
  • Keep records of contacts, agreements, and follow-ups.
  • Coordinate with the Treasurer, Service Coordinator, and other officers.

Good fit for someone who…

  • Enjoys meeting and contacting new people.
  • Writes and speaks professionally.
  • Can handle rejection without giving up.
  • Is organized about follow-ups.
  • Understands that partnerships should benefit both sides.

Time commitment

Around 2–4 hours most weeks. Some periods may be heavier during sponsorship and grant outreach.

Experience needed

No formal sales experience is required. Strong communication, professionalism, and persistence are important.

Media and Outreach

Media and Outreach helps people understand what MCA actually is. It should show the adventure, service, training, and friendships honestly without making the club look inaccessible or exaggerating its accomplishments.

Responsibilities

  • Photograph and document trips, service projects, training, and club events.
  • Manage social media and help update the website.
  • Create recruitment and promotional materials.
  • Collect member stories, trip reports, and photos.
  • Promote upcoming opportunities clearly and accurately.
  • Preserve a record of the club’s development over time.

Good fit for someone who…

  • Enjoys photography, writing, video, design, or social media.
  • Notices meaningful moments.
  • Can communicate consistently.
  • Wants to help new members feel welcome.
  • Is willing to attend and document different types of events.

Time commitment

Around 2–4 hours most weeks. More during recruitment periods and major events.

Experience needed

No professional media experience is required. Examples of past creative work are useful but not necessary.

Leadership Expectations

  • Prioritize member safety.
  • Lead with humility and integrity.
  • Be honest about your experience and limitations.
  • Teach what you know and continue learning.
  • Support fellow leaders and members.
  • Follow through on your responsibilities.
  • Create opportunities for others.
  • Leave places better than you found them.
  • Leave useful documentation for future leaders.

Not sure which role is right for you?

Leadership doesn’t always start with experience. If you’re interested in helping out but aren’t sure where you’d fit, I’d be happy to talk through the different roles and help you find one that matches your interests.

Joining MCA

Common FAQs

Welcome to MCA’s website! First and foremost, MCA is a community. The club is centered around pushing ourselves mentally and physically in the mountains, but we also aim to support our members in all of their pursuits.

What kind of gear do I need?

You don’t need any special equipment to come on MCA trips. All necessary trip equipment is provided, though at some point you may choose to purchase your own boots or other personal equipment.

What if I don’t have any backpacking or mountaineering experience?

No worries. The point of this club is to decrease the barriers to outdoor activities and help more people go on sick adventures.

What is the difference between the Arizona State Outdoors Club and the Mountaineering Club at Arizona State University?

You should absolutely do both. MCA focuses more on larger objectives that are often out of state, while the Arizona State Outdoors Club offers a wider variety of outings that mostly stay in Arizona. MCA still goes on plenty of smaller trips for fun, skill-building, and team bonding.

What does the time commitment look like?

The amount of time you spend on trips, training, meetings, and social events is flexible. Every member is expected to contribute to at least one MCA project by organizing something, joining a project team, or helping another member carry their idea out. For more advanced objectives, participants will also need to complete enough preparation for leaders to confirm that everyone is ready.

How much do trips cost?

All of our day trips are free. For trips over school breaks, participants split shared costs such as gas, flights, and food. On the most advanced trips, rentals for specialized boots, crampons, and ice axes should generally cost no more than $50 per person. When an objective does not require that specialized equipment, MCA should have the necessary group equipment available for participants.
